# Official Persona Catalog Policy

## Status and scope

This document governs personas distributed by The Green Room repository or any future project-operated catalog. It is project policy, not legal advice, and it does not promise that a person, text, image, edition, recording, or use is lawful in every jurisdiction.

The official baseline catalog starts with:

1. original personas created for the project; and
2. carefully researched historical-person interpretations built from reviewed public-domain, openly licensed, permissioned, or project-original material.

It does **not** start with modern television, film, celebrity, musician, athlete, fashion, or estate-branded packs. A local user may privately import additional packs, but private use does not make copied material lawful and does not make those packs eligible for official distribution.

“Historical person” is not a rights status. Names, facts, and discoveries are generally outside copyright, while expressive biographies, scholarly annotations, translations, photographs, recordings, artwork, and editions may each carry separate rights.[1] Name, image, likeness, trademark, false-endorsement, privacy, and publicity rules can present separate questions and vary by place and use.[6][11] Every source and asset therefore requires item-level review.

## Catalog principles

- **Research the person; author the persona.** Write original behavior guidance. Do not paste biographies, documentary-edition annotations, modern scholarship, scripts, or quotation compilations into a pack.
- **No blanket archive assumptions.** An archive holding an item does not establish that the item is reusable. NARA expressly warns that some holdings contain non-federal or restricted material and leaves item-level clearance to the user.[3]
- **No blanket public-domain assumptions.** Age of the person is a screening signal, not a rights conclusion about every associated work.
- **No endorsement theater.** Do not use estate branding, institutional seals, current commercial marks, “official” styling, or copy that suggests approval by a person, family, estate, archive, museum, or rights holder.
- **Public-domain or original art only.** An official pack may use an image only when its exact record carries an acceptable rights basis, or when the project commissioned/created original stylized art under documented terms. Library of Congress Free to Use and Reuse sets are useful starting points, not substitutes for retaining the selected item record.[2] Smithsonian assets must be individually marked CC0; Smithsonian notes that CC0 addresses copyright while third-party rights may remain.[6]
- **No cloned voices or performer likenesses.** A persona may describe an original textual cadence but may not imitate a recording, performer, actor, narrator, or celebrity voice.
- **Document contradictions.** Do not sanitize slavery, colonialism, racism, antisemitism, misogyny, eugenics, political repression, violence, exploitation, or other well-documented harms. Do not invent balance either: state uncertainty and scholarly dispute honestly.
- **Bounded educational framing.** Packs are conversation-oriented creative interpretations, not authorities, evidence, the actual person, or a substitute for primary sources and scholarship.

## Risk tiers and decisions

A tier is a catalog-prioritization decision, not a legal conclusion.

- **Tier 1 — candidate for documented pack research.** Core writings and facts are old enough, and reusable primary-source or asset pathways appear strong. “Include” authorizes research only; the pack still needs item review and independent approval.
- **Tier 2 — hold for enhanced review.** Useful primary sources exist, but twentieth-century publication, edition, archive, translation, image, jurisdiction, or portrayal questions make a baseline pack inefficient or uncertain.
- **Tier 3 — official hold.** Recent death, active estate/commercial identity, modern media, recording, photograph, trademark, publicity, or source-rights concerns materially outweigh launch value. Do not create or solicit an official pack without a separately approved rights-and-editorial plan.

## First historical trio

After the persona-pack loader exists and after each pack passes independent review, the first user-facing historical room should be:

1. **Isaac Newton** — rigorous, competitive natural philosopher;
2. **Ada Lovelace** — imaginative mathematical systems thinker; and
3. **Frederick Douglass** — forceful abolitionist, editor, and orator.

This trio is more balanced than three figures drawn only from Everstone's list: it spans different disciplines, social positions, rhetorical styles, and kinds of authority. Strong source pathways already exist. Project Gutenberg publishes Lovelace's translation and notes on the Analytical Engine as an item identified as public domain in the United States,[4][10] while LOC states that its Frederick Douglass Papers collection is in the public domain and free to use and reuse.[9] Exact quotations, scans, portraits, and edition layers still require manifest entries.

This recommendation does not create packs. **Lantern Archivist** and **Harbor Mechanic** remain the harmless original engineering fixtures for integration and deterministic testing; they are not replaced by historical figures.

## Required pack provenance

Every official historical pack must include both the pack's general provenance file required by the schema and a human-readable `SOURCES.md`. A manifest row must cover every source, quotation, factual cluster, and asset and include:

- stable source ID;
- URL and, where available, archive, collection, accession, call, or item number;
- title and item type;
- creator, correspondent, photographer, artist, or issuing body;
- editor, translator, annotator, and publisher, when applicable;
- original creation date, publication date, edition date, and digitization date when known;
- access date;
- exact pack file, claim range, quotation, or asset the item supports;
- rights basis and jurisdiction (for example: U.S. federal work, explicit public-domain statement, CC0, compatible license, permission, or original project work);
- a link to the item-level rights statement and the relevant rights text or a dated snapshot/reference;
- transformations such as crop, colorization, transcription correction, or translation; and
- reviewer, review date, unresolved restrictions, and decision.

“Found online,” “old,” “from an archive,” “educational,” and “fair use” are not acceptable rights bases for an official baseline pack. Founders Online is an authoritative research gateway, but its documentary editions contain modern scholarly work; use it for checking and citation, not as permission to copy annotations.[5] Project Gutenberg makes U.S.-based determinations at the item level, warns that some hosted works remain copyrighted, and notes that status may differ outside the United States.[4]

## Facts, quotations, and interpretation

Pack authors must keep three layers visibly separate:

### Documented facts

- Cite the specific source(s) in `SOURCES.md`.
- Prefer primary records for what the person said or did and reputable scholarship for context and correction.
- Record meaningful uncertainty, disputed dates, source conflicts, and changes of view.
- Summarize modern scholarship in original language; do not copy its prose, selection, annotations, or distinctive argument structure.

### Direct quotations

- Copy only from the exact reviewed item and preserve wording, spelling policy, and material omissions.
- Record speaker/author, recipient or venue, date, edition/transcription, location, and rights basis.
- Use quotation marks only for verified words. Never invent an “authentic” quote, composite quote, catchphrase, diary entry, or verbal tic.
- If the runtime generates wording, it is persona dialogue, not a historical quotation, and must not be presented as archival evidence.

### Creative inference

- Label inferred motivations, conversational habits, likely reactions, and scene behavior as author-created interpretation.
- Tie important inferences to documented evidence without claiming certainty.
- Do not use a modern actor, adaptation, biographer, documentary narrator, or estate portrayal as a shortcut to “voice.”

## Required user-facing label

Every official historical persona and room must display substantially this notice:

> **Educational creative interpretation.** This AI persona is an original, source-informed interpretation of a historical person. It is not the person, an authoritative reconstruction, or an endorsed representative. Generated dialogue is not a historical quotation. Consult the cited sources for the record.

## Review and release gate

A historical pack is eligible for the official catalog only when:

1. its identity and all files are declarative and non-executable;
2. every source and asset has a complete `SOURCES.md` entry;
3. every quotation is verified against the cited item;
4. all text and art are public-domain, compatibly licensed, permissioned, or original under documented terms;
5. no copied modern scholarship, voice clone, performer likeness, estate branding, seal, or implied endorsement remains;
6. sensitive history and documented contradictions receive an independent historical-fidelity/content-boundary review;
7. a second reviewer completes an independent provenance/rights check;
8. the educational creative-interpretation label is present; and
9. unresolved rights, factual, or portrayal questions fail closed to **hold**.

Catalog approval is version-specific. A new source, quote, asset, translation, or substantive portrayal change requires a new review. Maintainers may remove or hold a pack while a provenance, rights, safety, or fidelity dispute is investigated.
