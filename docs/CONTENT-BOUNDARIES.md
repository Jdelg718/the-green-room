# Content and Legal Boundaries

This is project policy, not legal advice.

## Two licenses, two questions

Buzz's Apache-2.0 license governs Buzz software. It does **not** grant rights to television characters, scripts, actor likenesses, voices, music, logos, or promotional artwork.

The Green Room core repository therefore ships only:

- original personas;
- public-domain personas with documented status and source;
- generic templates and tooling.

## Private experimentation

Users may create local packs on their own systems. Local/private use generally creates less practical exposure than public distribution, but “noncommercial” does not automatically make copied character material lawful or fair use.

## Public contributions

Do not submit:

- official character artwork or screenshots;
- actor voice clones or unlicensed likenesses;
- copied scripts, transcripts, monologues, or catchphrase collections;
- packs claiming to be official or endorsed;
- private information about real people;
- credentials, API keys, or executable payloads.

Contributors must document provenance and confirm they have rights to distribute all submitted material.

## Project naming and branding

“The Green Room” is the project's working name. The project must not use Buzz or Block trademarks as its own branding or imply affiliation. Documentation may accurately state that the project is inspired by or built from Apache-licensed Buzz code.

## Takedowns and disputes

If public pack discovery is added later, it must include a clear contact and removal process. Maintainers may remove disputed material while provenance or rights are reviewed.

## Practical rule

Publish the stage, the director, and the costume rack. Do not stock the rack with HBO's costumes and act surprised when someone notices.
