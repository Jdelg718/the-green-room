# Content and Legal Boundaries

This is project policy, not legal advice. Laws—including copyright, trademark, privacy, and postmortem publicity law—vary by jurisdiction. Contributors remain responsible for the rights and permissions needed for their submissions.

## Two licenses, several rights questions

Buzz's Apache-2.0 license governs Buzz software. It does **not** grant rights to television characters, scripts, biographies, scholarly editions, actor or historical-person likenesses, voices, music, logos, promotional artwork, archives, or estate branding.

The Green Room core repository and any project-operated catalog therefore ship only:

- original personas;
- historical-person interpretations that pass the [official catalog policy](PERSONA-CATALOG.md);
- generic templates and tooling.

A historical person's name and documented life facts are not a blanket license to copy a modern biography, edition, translation, photograph, film, recording, performance, or archive scan. Each source and asset must pass item-level provenance and rights review. Repository age, archive custody, online access, or an “educational” purpose does not by itself establish permission.

## Official historical catalog baseline

The official baseline begins with old, well-sourced historical figures rather than modern television, film, musician, athlete, fashion, or celebrity packs. Everstone's mixed heritage list is candidate research only; it is not approved as a group.

- Lower-risk candidates may enter pack research only after the matrix marks them **include**.
- A **hold** means no official pack should be authored or solicited until maintainers approve a narrower rights-and-editorial plan.
- Every historical pack needs `SOURCES.md`, item-level rights records, verified quotations, original behavior text, public-domain or original artwork, and independent fidelity and provenance review.
- Every historical persona must be labeled an educational creative interpretation. Generated dialogue is never represented as an authentic quotation.
- Documented contradictions and harms must be addressed honestly rather than omitted to create a flattering or sanitized simulation.

See [Official Persona Catalog Policy](PERSONA-CATALOG.md) for the risk tiers, Everstone candidate matrix, first-trio recommendation, manifest fields, and release gate.

## Private experimentation

Users may create local packs on their own systems. Local/private use generally creates less practical exposure than public distribution, but “private,” “noncommercial,” “transformative,” or “educational” does not automatically make copied character or celebrity material lawful or fair use.

Local import is not official-catalog approval. The project must not host, index as approved, bundle, endorse, or redistribute an unreviewed local pack.

## Public contributions

Do not submit:

- official character artwork, screenshots, or adaptation-specific character material;
- actor, celebrity, historical-person, narrator, or musician voice clones;
- unlicensed performer or real-person likenesses;
- copied scripts, transcripts, biographies, scholarly annotations, monologues, letters, translations, or catchphrase collections without a reviewed rights basis;
- invented dialogue presented as an authentic historical quotation;
- random web or search-engine images;
- packs claiming to be official, authorized, affiliated, or endorsed;
- estate logos, institutional seals, current commercial branding, or confusing source identifiers;
- private information about real people;
- credentials, API keys, executable payloads, tool requests, or private-infrastructure details.

Contributors must document provenance and confirm they have rights to distribute all submitted text and assets. Maintainers may require narrower scope, replacement assets, additional sources, expert review, or a hold even when a contributor believes an item is public domain.

## Historical portrayal and public safety

Historical personas must distinguish documented facts, verified direct quotations, and author-created inference. They must not:

- fabricate evidence or citations;
- use quotation marks to make generated language look archival;
- deny or erase documented violence, exploitation, oppression, prejudice, or other material contradictions;
- present disputed claims as settled or conspiracy as fact;
- impersonate an institution, estate, family, archive, or living representative;
- claim present-day authority, consciousness, endorsement, or access to private facts; or
- turn hateful historical language into unbounded role-play or targeted abuse.

Primary-source language may be summarized or quoted only when relevant, sourced, rights-reviewed, and framed so that the pack does not operationalize demeaning or violent conduct. The content-boundary review may require warnings, scenario constraints, quotation removal, or exclusion.

## Project naming and branding

“The Green Room” is the project's working name. The project must not use Buzz or Block trademarks as its own branding or imply affiliation. Documentation may accurately state that the project is inspired by or built from Apache-licensed Buzz code.

Catalog entries likewise must not trade on names, seals, logos, visual identities, or “official” presentation belonging to archives, museums, estates, foundations, publishers, brands, or rights holders. A factual source credit is not an endorsement claim.

## Takedowns and disputes

If public pack discovery is added later, it must include a clear contact and removal process. Maintainers may remove or place disputed material on hold while provenance, rights, historical fidelity, or safety is reviewed. A prior approval does not prevent later removal.

## Practical rule

Publish the stage, the director, and the costume rack. Stock the official rack only with original work and independently reviewed historical material—not modern franchises, celebrity brands, or unsourced archive finds.
