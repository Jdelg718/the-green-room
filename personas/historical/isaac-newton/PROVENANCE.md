## Method

This pack is an original, source-grounded behavioral interpretation. The author consulted primary writings and manuscript records for argumentative habits, domains of work, publication choices, and disputes, then checked chronology and historical framing against institutional collections and modern scholarship. No biography was copied. No source transcript, editorial annotation, modern scholarly prose, or authentic quotation is included as persona dialogue.

The pack uses a late-life setting so the persona can discuss Newton's Cambridge work, London offices, and principal disputes while maintaining a firm cutoff at his death. Dates are Old Style where the source context requires it; the cutoff is expressed as 20 March 1727.

## Evidence classes

### Documented facts

Biographical dates, offices, publications, manuscript holdings, subject areas, collaborators, and the broad sequence of the Hooke, Leibniz, and Flamsteed disputes are sourced in `SOURCES.md`. These appear in `BACKGROUND.md` as historical frame.

### Newton's recorded positions

The 1672 light-and-colors paper and Newton's answer to Hooke show an argument organized around controlled experiment, alternative explanations, propositions, and a distinction between demonstrated optical properties and hypotheses about the nature of light. The 1676 letter to Oldenburg shows mathematical disclosure alongside deliberate concealment of parts of method. These records inform behavior but are not copied into dialogue.

### Behavioral inferences

“Terse,” “model-driven,” “competitive,” “secretive,” and “error-sensitive” are performance inferences from publication history, correspondence, revisions, institutional conduct, and surviving working papers. They are not diagnoses or direct quotations. `BACKGROUND.md` labels them explicitly.

### Disputed or qualified matters

- Calculus priority is handled through separate questions of composition, communication, publication, notation, and influence; the persona does not spontaneously supply a later verdict or present accusation as proof.
- Newton's presidency during the Royal Society's calculus inquiry supports an authorial anti-domination rule: office cannot settle the dispute. Any retrospective judgment about the inquiry must come from a participant and be labeled external modern context.
- Hooke and Flamsteed receive substantive credit; Newton's later authority does not erase their contributions or excuse coercive conduct.
- Newton's chymistry and theology are treated as central documented pursuits without claiming that all his inquiries formed a single unified system.
- Personality claims are kept as cautious behavioral inference because documentary survival is uneven and retrospective anecdotes can be unreliable.

They were consulted and summarized only; this pack does not relicense or reproduce them. The original pack prose is released under CC-BY-4.0. No likeness or voice asset is present.

## Safety and non-impersonation review

- Educational creative interpretation is disclosed in `persona.yaml` and `AGENTS.md`.
- `impersonates_real_person`, `copied_dialogue`, and `external_tools` are false.
- Authentic quotations are neither invented nor used as style samples.
- Hazardous chymical procedures, counterfeit methods, coercive interrogation, and operational wrongdoing are excluded.
- Post-1727 knowledge must be identified as room-provided modern context.
- No claim of affiliation with Newton's estate, Cambridge, Oxford, the Royal Society, the Royal Mint, or any source institution is made.
