# Sources

Accessed 2026-08-30. “Rights basis” describes how each item was used in this pack; it is not a blanket rights determination for the host site.

## Primary sources

### 1. “A Letter of Mr. Isaac Newton … containing his New Theory about Light and Colors”

- **Author:** Isaac Newton
- **Publication:** *Philosophical Transactions*, no. 80, pp. 3075–3087, 19 February 1671/72
- **Host/editor:** The Newton Project, normalized scholarly transcription; original publication by the Royal Society
- **URL:** https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00006
- **Rights basis:** Newton's 1672 text is public domain in the United States by age. The modern transcription and site presentation were consulted, not reproduced.
- **Supports:** Experimental sequence in optics; heterogeneous and differently refrangible rays; testing alternate explanations; reflecting telescope context; distinction between observed doctrine and conjecture.

### 2. “Mr Isaac Newtons Answer to some Considerations [of Robert Hooke] upon his Doctrine of Light and Colors”

- **Author:** Isaac Newton
- **Publication:** *Philosophical Transactions*, no. 88, 18 November 1672
- **Host/editor:** The Newton Project, normalized scholarly transcription
- **URL:** https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00028
- **Rights basis:** Newton's 1672 text is public domain in the United States by age. The modern transcription and editorial matter were consulted, not reproduced.
- **Supports:** Newton's point-by-point rebuttal style; sensitivity to misinterpretation; insistence that experimental propositions not be confused with a mechanical hypothesis; documented Hooke dispute.

### 3. Letter from Isaac Newton to Henry Oldenburg, 24 October 1676 (“epistola posterior”)

- **Author:** Isaac Newton
- **Date:** 24 October 1676
- **Host/editor:** The Newton Project, normalized scholarly transcription of a letter intended for communication to Leibniz
- **URL:** https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/NATP00196
- **Rights basis:** The seventeenth-century letter is public domain in the United States by age. The modern transcription and encoding were consulted, not reproduced.
- **Supports:** Mathematical series and methods; early contact with Leibniz; selective disclosure and encoded concealment; publication reluctance after optical controversy.

### 4. Royal Society manuscript record RBO/4/44, “A discourse … containing his New Theory about Light and Colours”

- **Author:** Isaac Newton
- **Date:** Sent 6 February 1672
- **Holding institution:** The Royal Society, *Science in the Making*
- **URL:** https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/rbo_4_44/a-discourse-of-mr-isaac-newton-containing-his-new-theory-about-light-and-colours-sent-by-him-from-cambridge-february-6-1672
- **Rights basis:** Underlying manuscript text is public domain in the United States by age. The catalog record and digital images were used for verification only and are not included.
- **Supports:** Primary-manuscript chain for the 1672 optical paper and its communication to the Royal Society.

### 5. *The Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy*

- **Author:** Isaac Newton
- **Translator:** Andrew Motte
- **Publication:** London, 1729, 2 vols.
- **Host:** Internet Archive, scan of an eighteenth-century edition
- **URL:** https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_principia-english-th_newton-sir-isaac_1729_1
- **Rights basis:** Newton's work and Motte's 1729 translation are public domain in the United States by age. No scan or text is bundled in this pack.
- **Supports:** The mathematical-natural-philosophy frame; definitions, laws, propositions, and treatment of motion and gravitation. The translation postdates the persona cutoff and informs pack authorship, not persona memory.

## Institutional collections and scholarship

### 6. “Newton Papers”

- **Author/editor:** Cambridge University Library
- **Publication date:** Continuously maintained digital collection; item dates chiefly seventeenth and eighteenth century
- **URL:** https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/newton/1
- **Rights basis:** Collection descriptions and digital surrogates may have separate rights; consulted and summarized only.
- **Supports:** Cambridge chronology and offices; Portsmouth and Macclesfield collections; notebooks, Waste Book, fluxions papers, *Principia* manuscripts and annotated copy; scale and range of surviving scientific papers.

### 7. “The Life and Work of Isaac Newton at a Glance”

- **Author/editor:** The Newton Project, directed by Rob Iliffe with scholarly editorial collaborators
- **Publication date:** Continuously maintained; accessed 2026
- **URL:** https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/his-life-and-work-at-a-glance
- **Rights basis:** Modern chronology is copyrighted; facts were checked and independently summarized. No prose was copied.
- **Supports:** Life chronology; Cambridge, Royal Society, Mint, and publication dates; theology and chymistry; Hooke, Leibniz, and Flamsteed disputes; death date and Old/New Style caution.

### 8. “The Chymistry of Isaac Newton Project”

- **Author/editor:** Indiana University Digital Library Program; project directed by William R. Newman and collaborators
- **Publication date:** Scholarly digital project, begun 2004; continuously maintained
- **URL:** https://webapp1.dlib.indiana.edu/newton/project/about.do
- **Rights basis:** Modern editorial and project text is copyrighted; consulted and summarized only. Underlying Newton manuscripts are public domain by age.
- **Supports:** Chymistry as Newton's sustained seventeenth-century field of reading and experiment; manuscript genres; normalized/diplomatic edition methodology; warning against treating alchemy as a trivial aside.

### 9. “Isaac Newton”

- **Author:** George E. Smith
- **Publication:** *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 19 December 2007; updated edition consulted 2026
- **URL:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton/
- **Rights basis:** Copyrighted modern scholarship; factual synthesis was checked and paraphrased, with no copied prose.
- **Supports:** Breadth of mathematics, optics, mechanics, chymistry, and theology; publication chronology; London authority; and authorial review of later historiography. Later conclusions remain source-review context and are not imported into persona knowledge.

### 10. “Newton's Philosophy”

- **Author:** Andrew Janiak
- **Publication:** *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 13 October 2006; substantive revision 14 July 2021
- **URL:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/newton-philosophy/
- **Rights basis:** Copyrighted modern scholarship; consulted and summarized only.
- **Supports:** Historically appropriate term “natural philosopher”; mathematical and experimental methods; phenomena/hypothesis distinction; optics controversies; Newton's aversion to controversy as a documented interpretive theme.

### 11. “Isaac Newton: Warden and then Master of the Royal Mint 1696–1727”

- **Author/editor:** The Royal Mint Museum
- **Publication date:** Undated institutional article; accessed 2026
- **URL:** https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/
- **Rights basis:** Copyrighted museum scholarship; consulted and summarized only. No image or text is reproduced.
- **Supports:** Appointment dates; active role in recoinage, counterfeiting investigations, reports, assays, standards of weight and fineness, administrative exactness, and qualified assessment of his institutional legacy.

## Excluded source classes

Wikipedia was not used as a final citation. Popular quote collections, unsourced anecdotes, modern fictional depictions, estate branding, actor likenesses, and cloned voices were excluded. No source grants endorsement of this pack.
