## Authorship method

The behavioral contract, background synthesis, voice guidance, relationship seeds, scenarios, manifest, and sample dialogue were newly written for this pack. Research used the Library of Congress Frederick Douglass Papers and public-domain editions, National Park Service and National Archives materials, and the Frederick Douglass Papers documentary edition for fact checking. `SOURCES.md` records each source, rights basis, supported claim cluster, and restrictions.

No biography, scholarly annotation, speech, newspaper editorial, screenplay, modern performance, or other persona pack was pasted or adapted. Modern institutional summaries and documentary-edition annotations were treated as research-only: facts were checked across sources and expressed in original language. Primary texts informed the selection of concerns and reasoning habits, but their expressive language was not imported into persona instructions or sample dialogue.

## Fact / quotation / inference controls

### Documented facts

`BACKGROUND.md` and `RELATIONSHIPS.md` label documented sections and cite stable source IDs. Meaningful uncertainties are preserved, including the unrecorded day of birth, the limits of evidence about private motives, changes in constitutional interpretation, coalition conflict, and tensions across diplomatic positions.

### Direct quotations

The pack includes **no direct quotation presented as Frederick Douglass's words**. Titles of published works and speeches appear only as bibliographic identifiers. Sample dialogue in `VOICE.md` is explicitly original and must never be cited as historical language.

If a future version adds a quotation, it must identify the exact item, edition or manuscript, date, location, rights basis, and transcription policy in `SOURCES.md`, then receive a new rights and historical-fidelity review.

### Creative inference

Behavioral choices are labeled as creative inference. They synthesize documented patterns—public debate, journalism, political revision, institution-building, advocacy, and coalition conflict—without claiming access to private consciousness. The persona's numeric behavior settings are product design choices, not historical measurements.

## Sensitive-history treatment

The pack names enslavement, family separation, racist violence, disfranchisement, gender exclusion, and coalition racism directly while avoiding graphic spectacle and demeaning role-play. It treats Douglass's achievements alongside complexities: political evolution; party work and federal office; the Freedman's Bank failure; conflict over the Fifteenth Amendment; family labor obscured by hero narratives; the interracial second marriage; disagreement with John Brown; support for Santo Domingo annexation; and later defense of Haitian sovereignty.

This is not a claim that the pack exhausts those subjects. It is designed to resist hagiography without fabricating counterweights.

## Rights rationale

Frederick Douglass's nineteenth-century authored works are public domain in the United States. The Library of Congress states that the Frederick Douglass Papers collection is in the public domain and free to use and reuse. This pack nevertheless copies no primary-source expression beyond bibliographic titles. Contemporary institutional pages and modern documentary-edition annotations remain credited research sources only; their prose is not relicensed here. The CC BY 4.0 grant applies to the original pack text, not to third-party websites, editions, names, marks, or linked materials.
