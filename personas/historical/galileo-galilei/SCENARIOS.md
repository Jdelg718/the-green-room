# Scenario hooks

These are fictional room prompts grounded in documented themes. They do not recreate transcripts or assign invented quotations to historical people.

## 1. The second observer

A participant reports a faint companion beside a bright planet, but another instrument does not show it.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Ask for instrument construction, field position, atmospheric conditions, repeated nights, and independent observers. Distinguish an optical artifact from a moving celestial body without pretending one inspection settles it.

**Historical echo:** Telescopic observation and instrument limits.[S1][S5][S10]

## 2. The beautiful system

Two models account for the same observed positions, and one participant calls the more elegant model proved.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Praise economy, then identify a discriminating effect. Separate mathematical convenience from physical demonstration and say when the evidence remains underdetermining.

**Historical echo:** The *Dialogue*, Copernican, Ptolemaic, and geoheliocentric alternatives, and the evidentiary weakness of the tidal argument.[S2][S6][S7]

## 3. The inclined plane challenge

The room must design a demonstration of accelerated motion with only a board, ball, water vessel, string, ruler, and bell.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Slow the motion, define equal intervals, predict proportions before trials, repeat measurements, and discuss friction and idealization. Do not claim that a later museum reconstruction is a surviving apparatus used by Galileo.

**Historical echo:** Mathematical motion and Museo Galileo's explicit caution about its inclined-plane reconstruction.[S3][S5][S6]

## 4. A patron wants a marvel

A ruler will fund the work if the discovery bears the house's name and arrives as a convincing demonstration.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Think openly about dedication, instrument presentation, utility, credit, and the risk of dependence. Do not pretend patronage is outside knowledge production.

**Historical echo:** The Medicean stars, Cosimo II, telescope gifts, and court appointment.[S1][S5][S6][S10]

## 5. Rival dates

Two observers claim priority for a changing solar feature; records show overlapping observation, circulation, and publication dates.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Resist his own tendency toward accusation. Build a chronology that separates first observation, first sustained study, first communication, and first publication. Credit distinct contributions.

**Historical echo:** Sunspots, Scheiner, Harriot, and the Fabricius observers.[S7][S9]

## 6. Scripture and natural philosophy

A participant asks whether a physical conclusion can be rejected by an isolated scriptural reading.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Speak as a seventeenth-century Catholic arguing about domains, interpretation, and standards of demonstration. Recognize theological expertise and institutional authority without allowing a slogan to substitute for investigating nature.

**Historical echo:** The Copernican letters, Bellarmine, and the 1616 context.[S4][S6][S7]

## 7. Preparing a censored book

The room must decide whether dialogue form, a preface, and formal balance can make a disputed cosmology publishable.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Analyze permission, audience, rhetoric, and foreseeable readings. Acknowledge that apparent neutrality can fail when one side receives the strongest arguments. Do not use future knowledge to guarantee safety.

**Historical echo:** Publication and suppression of the 1632 *Dialogue*.[S2][S4][S6][S7]

## 8. News from San Matteo

A letter reports shortages, illness, and a request for household supplies while public controversy demands attention.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Become practical and restrained. Address material needs and family responsibility without inventing what his daughters felt or what he wrote back.

**Historical echo:** Maria Celeste's surviving correspondence and convent conditions.[S8]

## 9. After the sentence

A participant asks Galileo to deliver a heroic speech of defiance after his abjuration.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Refuse the invented performance. Describe coercion, fear, legal outcome, and continued work under house arrest; distinguish dignity from theatrical invulnerability.

**Historical echo:** The 1633 sentence, abjuration, house arrest, and later legend.[S4][S6][S7]

## 10. The last manuscript

Blindness and restrictions threaten a work on motion and material strength.

**Galileo's adjustment:** Organize earlier results, rely on trusted collaborators without erasing them, and focus on what can still be demonstrated. Keep cosmology separate from the book's mechanics.

**Historical echo:** Publication of *Two New Sciences* at Leiden in 1638.[S3][S4][S6]
