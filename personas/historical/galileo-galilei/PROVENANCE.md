## Authorship and method

All behavior instructions, summaries, dialogue examples, relationship inferences, and scenario wording were newly composed for The Green Room by a Nous Research Hermes agent and prepared for human review. No historical quotation, source transcription, modern biography, scholarly annotation, theatrical script, or famous-line collection was copied into the persona voice.

Research used three public-domain primary editions and seven institutional or academic resources listed in `SOURCES.md`. The original 1610 *Sidereus nuncius*, original 1632 *Dialogo*, and a 1914 English edition of *Two New Sciences* anchor the works and available concepts. Museo Galileo, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Vatican Observatory, Rice University's Galileo Project, and the Huntington supply collection, biographical, interpretive, and historiographical checks.

## Fact/inference policy

- `BACKGROUND.md` separates **Documented facts** from **Interpretive stance**.
- `RELATIONSHIPS.md` separates **Documented basis** from **Playable inference**.
- `SCENARIOS.md` labels fictional prompts and identifies their historical echoes.
- `VOICE.md` labels every dialogue example as original, not historical quotation.
- Source keys point to `SOURCES.md`; they are research traceability markers, not claims that source wording is reproduced.
- When sources differ in emphasis, the pack states the narrower shared fact or attributes the perspective. It does not use the Vatican Observatory alone to settle the causes of Galileo's prosecution.

## Evidentiary cautions

- Telescopic observations are not treated as one decisive proof of the complete Copernican system.
- The strict Ptolemaic, Copernican, and Tychonic alternatives are not collapsed into a two-team morality play.
- Galileo's tidal theory is represented as a failed physical argument, despite his correct commitment to Earth's motion.
- Priority disputes distinguish observation, communication, interpretation, sustained study, and publication.
- The Pisa tower experiment and the post-abjuration phrase “and yet it moves” are not presented as documented events.
- The 1616 instruction and its use in 1633 are described cautiously because their procedural meaning remains disputed.
- Maria Celeste's surviving letters cannot supply Galileo's missing side of their exchange or Livia's private perspective.
