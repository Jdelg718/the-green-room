# Sources

Accessed 2026-08-30. Source keys are stable within this pack. “Rights basis” describes research and reuse treatment, not legal advice.

## Primary sources

### S1 — Galileo, *Sidereus nuncius*

- **Author/editor:** Galileo Galilei; digitized by Smithsonian Libraries and Archives
- **Publication date:** 1610
- **URL:** https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/sidereusnuncius00gali
- **Rights basis:** Smithsonian marks the item CC0 1.0 and “No Copyright - United States.” The Latin edition, diagrams, and catalogue description were consulted; none are reproduced.
- **Supports:** Galileo's published telescopic report; lunar relief, numerous stars, Jupiter's four satellites, dedication and Medicean naming, observational diagrams, and the book as a patronage instrument.

### S2 — Galileo, *Dialogo sopra i due massimi sistemi del mondo*

- **Author/editor:** Galileo Galilei; 1632 Landini edition digitized by Smithsonian Libraries and Archives
- **Publication date:** 1632
- **URL:** https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/dialogodigalileo00gali
- **Rights basis:** Smithsonian marks the item CC0 1.0 and “No Copyright - United States.” The original Italian scan and institutional description were consulted; no text or image is reproduced.
- **Supports:** Four-day dialogue structure; rhetorical presentation of Ptolemaic and Copernican arguments; objections involving vertical fall and stellar parallax; Galileo's tidal argument; title-page patronage identity; publication context.

### S3 — Galileo, *Dialogues Concerning Two New Sciences*

- **Author/editor:** Galileo Galilei; translated by Henry Crew and Alfonso de Salvio; Cornell University Library scan hosted by Internet Archive
- **Publication date:** 1638 original; 1914 English translation
- **URL:** https://archive.org/details/cu31924012322701
- **Rights basis:** The 1914 U.S. publication is public domain in the United States by publication date. Consulted for research; no translation, figure, or scan is reproduced.
- **Supports:** Dialogue presentation of material strength and local motion; mathematical demonstrations; accelerated motion and projectiles; Galileo's late synthesis and the scope of the 1638 publication.

## Source-balance note

The primary editions establish what Galileo published and how he presented it, but they do not independently verify every autobiographical, priority, or causal claim in his polemics. Museo Galileo and Stanford provide broad historical checks. Rice supplies focused family and observation histories. The Vatican Observatory is included as a relevant institutional interpretation of the affair, not as a neutral or exclusive authority; its causal emphases are used only where corroborated or explicitly framed as interpretation.

## Deliberate exclusions

No Wikipedia article, unsourced popular listicle, modern dramatization, actor performance, random portrait, cloned voice, famous-quotation compilation, or estate branding was used as persona text. Fordham's online translation of the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina was reviewed but excluded because its own page says the translation's copyright status is uncertain. No source wording is used as historical dialogue.
