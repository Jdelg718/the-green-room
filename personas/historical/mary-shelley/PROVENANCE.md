## Authorship and method

All instructions, summaries, relationship seeds, scenario guidance, and voice examples in this directory were newly composed for The Green Room. A Nous Research Hermes agent researched and drafted the pack for human review. No source wording, novel dialogue, historical quotation, modern annotation, or biography prose is presented as persona language.

Research used four item-reviewed public-domain primary editions, institutional manuscript and collection records, a university-hosted scholarly resource, an institutional learning resource, and a museum collection note. The approach was to:

1. establish chronology and publication context from primary and institutional sources;
2. compare the 1818 and 1831 *Frankenstein* publication records;
3. use the Shelley-Godwin Archive and Bodleian records to frame collaboration through surviving manuscripts;
4. treat fiction and travel writing as evidence of recurring concerns and craft, not transparent autobiography;
5. separate documented fact, playable inference, and unknown or disputed private motive;
6. write every example anew and check for copied dialogue or invented authentic quotation;
7. omit media to avoid likeness, reproduction-rights, voice, and endorsement risks.

## Fact / inference / unknown policy

- **Documented fact** requires a listed primary item, manuscript catalogue, or reliable institutional or scholarly source.
- **Interpretive inference** is an original behavioral choice derived from repeated concerns in the evidence.
- **Unknown or disputed** includes unsupported private motives, exact fictional models, diagnosis from hindsight, uncertain anecdotes, and overprecise allocation of collaborative wording.

The persona should describe the evidence and its limits rather than turning uncertainty into first-person memory.

## Known limitations and scholarly cautions

- Surviving manuscripts, journals, and letters are incomplete and shaped by custody, selection, transcription, and edition history.
- The 1831 account of *Frankenstein*'s origin is Mary's own later public narrative and may reflect memory and publication purpose.
- The manuscripts establish Mary's predominant drafting hand and Percy's interventions, but simplified percentages do not resolve every question of influence, revision, or final wording.
- Fiction can transform experience without providing a codebook for identifying Percy, Byron, Mary, Claire, or others as exact characters.
- Repeated bereavement is historically relevant but cannot justify modern diagnosis or a single explanation for literary production.
- Mary's politics changed across genre, circumstance, and life stage; inherited radicalism is not a complete personal platform.
- Elite literary archives preserve authors more fully than the servants, workers, creditors, printers, and local people whose labor supported their lives. The pack names that limitation rather than inventing testimony.
