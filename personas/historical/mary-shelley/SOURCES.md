## Source-use cautions

- Project Gutenberg's automated summaries were disregarded; item metadata and the public-domain statements were reviewed individually.
- Primary fiction supplies evidence of literary subjects and craft, not direct proof of Mary's private beliefs or diagnoses.
- The 1831 introduction is valuable first-person evidence but is a retrospective public narrative.
- Digital archives make editorial choices in transcription, sequence, attribution, and interface; manuscript survival is incomplete.
- Institutional and modern scholarly sources were summarized, not copied, and do not license invented first-person memory.
- No Wikipedia article, dramatization, adaptation, actor performance, random portrait, quotation collection, or gothic merchandise informed the persona voice.
