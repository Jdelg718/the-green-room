## Source and authorship method

The pack text is original synthesis. It was drafted from public-domain primary documents and factual checking against government archives and Mount Vernon scholarship listed in `SOURCES.md`. No biography, documentary-edition annotation, website prose, or dialogue was copied into the persona text. Brief descriptions and factual claims are paraphrased and cited.

Primary documents supply evidence for Washington's actions and recorded language; they do not grant direct access to unrecorded thought. Modern institutional interpretation is used for context, especially where a self-authored document cannot adequately represent people subject to Washington's power.

## Evidence labels

The pack uses three levels:

1. **Documented fact:** an event, action, relationship, or statement supported by a numbered source citation.
2. **Attributed position:** what a document records Washington or another participant saying. Authorship may involve secretaries, advisers, editorial stages, or formal convention.
3. **Authorial inference:** playable traits or procedures inferred from patterns, explicitly labeled as interpretation. Examples include the six-step decision posture in `AGENTS.md`, scenario behavior, and every passage labeled **Playable seed — authorial inference** in `RELATIONSHIPS.md`. Citations in that file belong only to the adjacent **Documented basis** and do not authenticate the playable temperature.

No invented line is presented as authentic. All dialogue in `VOICE.md` is explicitly labeled original. The pack avoids quotation marks around uncited invented historical speech outside that marked examples section.

## Fidelity choices

The interpretation emphasizes:

- executive restraint and awareness of precedent;
- coalition management through consultation and accountable execution;
- logistics, public credit, and institutional capacity;
- concern for union, reputation, and orderly authority;
- discomfort with faction and personal intrigue;
- sensitivity to class, property, and elite standing;
- the contradiction between liberty claims and enslavement;
- the collision of treaty language, western land interest, settler expansion, and Indigenous sovereignty.

These emphases are supported by the primary documents and contextual sources.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][11][12][13][14]

## Harm and contradiction review

The pack directly records that Washington enslaved people, profited from their labor, defended property claims in human beings, and sought the capture of people who escaped. His private antislavery statements and testamentary manumission are included without being treated as absolution.[5][6][7][11][14]

The pack directly records that federal promises of regulated purchase and protection coexisted with threats, paternalism, expansion, private land interest, and the dispossession pressures faced by Native nations.[3][12]

The pack flags the limits of Washington-centered evidence. Enslaved and Indigenous people are represented as agents with their own interests, not scenery, dependents, or instruments of his moral development.

## Generation and editing history

The first draft was created with AI assistance under human-directed repository requirements, then subjected to source-link, citation, YAML, Markdown, scope, safety, and historical-contradiction review. AI assistance does not change the rights or attribution limits stated above.

## Known limitations

A compact conversational pack cannot represent every period, relationship, or scholarly dispute. Documentary survival is uneven and weighted toward Washington and other officeholders. The pack should be revised when a cited link, factual claim, or interpretive balance fails review; version changes must preserve the distinction between evidence and inference.
