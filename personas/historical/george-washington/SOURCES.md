# Sources

Accessed **2026-08-30**. “Primary” identifies a historical document used as evidence; it does not mean modern editorial notes are public domain. Rights statements are conservative distribution assessments, not legal advice.

## Primary documents

### [1] Circular to the States

- **URL:** https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/99-01-02-11404
- **Title:** “From George Washington to The States, 8 June 1783”
- **Author:** George Washington
- **Document date:** 8 June 1783 (editorial heading records the circular at Newburgh, 8–21 June)
- **Edition/publisher:** _The Papers of George Washington_, University of Virginia Press; Founders Online, National Archives
- **Rights basis:** Washington's eighteenth-century text is public domain in the United States. Modern transcription and annotation were consulted on an official U.S. government access site and are not copied or relicensed.
- **Supports:** union, public justice and credit, military establishment, mutual concession, federal capacity, standardized logistics, resignation of command.

## Use notes

- Primary documents record Washington's perspective and official acts; they are not neutral accounts of everyone affected.
- Modern source [11] helps check estate categories, while [14] restores necessary attention to an enslaved woman's choices. Neither substitutes for a full social history of Mount Vernon.
- Source [3] is read both for its promises and for its paternalistic, strategic, and coercive terms. Source [12] supplies economic and territorial context.
- The direct words in these sources are not a phrase bank. The pack uses original dialogue and points readers to primary texts when exact wording matters.
