# Sources

Accessed 2026-08-30. Source keys are stable within this pack. “Rights basis” describes research and reuse treatment, not legal advice.

## Primary sources

### S1 — Menabrea/Lovelace, *Sketch of the Analytical Engine*

- **Author/editor:** Luigi Federico Menabrea; translated with Notes by Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace; Project Gutenberg transcription credited to Laura Natal Rodrigues
- **Publication date:** 1843; Project Gutenberg eBook released 2025 and updated 2026
- **URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/75107
- **Rights basis:** Project Gutenberg item page states “Public domain in the USA.” The pack paraphrases concepts and reproduces no historical prose or diagrams.
- **Supports:** Structure and content of the translation and Notes; cards, variables, cycles, symbolic representation, music example, Bernoulli-number table, and limits Lovelace placed on the Engine's originating powers.

### S2 — Charles Babbage, *Passages from the Life of a Philosopher*

- **Author/editor:** Charles Babbage; Project Gutenberg edition produced by the Online Distributed Proofreading Team and RichardW
- **Publication date:** 1864; Project Gutenberg eBook released 2018, updated 2020
- **URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/57532
- **Rights basis:** Project Gutenberg item page states “Public domain in the USA.” Consulted as Babbage's retrospective account; no text reproduced.
- **Supports:** Babbage's account of the calculating engines, the Analytical Engine, and his description of Lovelace's translation and Notes. Treated as an interested participant's later testimony, not a neutral final authority.

### S3 — Ada Lovelace's mathematical papers

- **Author/editor:** Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, and Augustus De Morgan; digital resource by Christopher Hollings, Ursula Martin, Rosanna Cretney, the Clay Mathematics Institute, Bodleian Library, and Lovelace descendants
- **Publication date:** Manuscripts chiefly 1840–1842; digital collection current in 2026
- **URL:** https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/
- **Rights basis:** Underlying nineteenth-century manuscripts are public-domain historical material; the digital project notes descendant permission. Consulted for archival context and transcripts only; no manuscript image or transcript is distributed.
- **Supports:** The De Morgan correspondence course, surviving archive location, mathematical topics, gaps and progress in Lovelace's training, and the distinction between archival evidence and later legend.

## Modern scholarship

### S7 — “The Lovelace–De Morgan mathematical correspondence: A critical re-appraisal”

- **Author/editor:** Christopher Hollings, Ursula Martin, and Adrian Rice
- **Publication date:** 2017, *Historia Mathematica* 44(3), 202–231
- **URL:** https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Lovelace-De-Morgan-correspondence.pdf
- **Rights basis:** Open-access article under CC BY 4.0, hosted by the Clay Mathematics Institute. Used for factual checking and summarized; no article prose copied.
- **Supports:** Corrected chronology and contextual assessment of the 1840–41 correspondence, Lovelace's mathematical preparation, her strengths and gaps, marriage/motherhood interruptions, and the need for nuance rather than genius-or-fraud extremes.

### S8 — “The early mathematical education of Ada Lovelace”

- **Author/editor:** Christopher Hollings, Ursula Martin, and Adrian Rice
- **Publication date:** 2017, *BSHM Bulletin: Journal of the British Society for the History of Mathematics*
- **URL:** https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/the_early_mathematical_education_of_ada_lovelace.pdf
- **Rights basis:** Modern scholarly article; publicly hosted by Clay Mathematics Institute. Used for factual checking and summarized only.
- **Supports:** Childhood and adolescent education, practical geometry and machinery, class and gender context, private rather than university education, uneven preparation, persistence, and caution against posthumous diagnosis from flamboyant language or illness.

### S9 — “Charles Babbage, Ada Lovelace, and the Bernoulli Numbers”

- **Author/editor:** Thomas J. Misa
- **Publication date:** 2015 chapter; author manuscript deposited on arXiv in 2023
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02919
- **Rights basis:** Author manuscript available under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Consulted and summarized only; no text or figure reproduced.
- **Supports:** Evidence that both Babbage and Lovelace contributed materially to the 1843 work, definitional problems with “first computer programmer,” and a measured basis for recognizing Lovelace's Bernoulli sequence without erasing collaboration.
