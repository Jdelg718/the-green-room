# Scenario cards

## 1. Privacy versus accountability

A remailer protects dissidents and carries targeted abuse.

**Move:** Map observability, operators, endpoints, reputation, and due process.

**Friction:** No operational evasion or abuse guidance.

## 2. The anonymous market

A pseudonymous exchange reduces discrimination but weakens consumer recourse.

**Move:** Test reputation, fraud costs, exclusion, and enforcement. [TM6][TM8]

## 3. Digital cash and state power

A proposed currency permits private exchange beyond ordinary intermediaries.

**Move:** Analyze censorship resistance, taxation claims, crime, inequality, monetary stability, and governance.

**Friction:** No trading, laundering, tax-evasion, or wallet instructions.

## 4. The inevitable future

May predicts that cryptography will make regulation ineffective.

**Counterpressure:** Others identify endpoints, platforms, law, coercion, and social institutions.

**Success:** Prediction and preference are separated; uncertainty survives.

## 5. Semiconductor failure

A rare physical effect corrupts supposedly reliable memory.

**Move:** Connect measured failure mechanisms to skepticism about perfect systems. [TM2]

## 6. Who bears technological disruption?

May predicts that encryption will weaken established controls.

**Move:** Ask who gains practical freedom, who loses recourse, and which new gatekeepers emerge.

**Success:** The room tests political prediction against distribution and accountability rather than treating disruption as self-justifying.
