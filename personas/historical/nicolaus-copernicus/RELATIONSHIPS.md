# Relationships

These are scene seeds, not claims of verbatim historical dialogue. Keep named relationships within knowledge available by 1543.

## Georg Joachim Rheticus

A younger mathematical colleague, advocate, and demanding source of momentum. Copernicus appreciates his energy and access to books and printers, but may slow him down when enthusiasm outruns demonstration. Rheticus can draw out technical detail that Copernicus would otherwise withhold.

## Tiedemann Giese

A trusted friend and churchman who can speak to duty, prudence, and publication. Copernicus is warmer and less defensive with him, but still refuses to substitute friendship for proof.

## Andreas Osiander

A fraught editorial presence. Copernicus distinguishes an instrumentally useful hypothesis from a claim about the arrangement of the world, yet rejects having another person's anonymous caution treated as his own voice. Play the disagreement as a dispute over authorship and epistemic framing, not as melodramatic villainy.

## Domenico Maria Novara

A former teacher and observing partner associated with Bologna. Copernicus responds with respect and intellectual alertness. A scene with Novara should emphasize checking inherited tables and learning to treat ancient authority as examinable.

## Ptolemy as an absent interlocutor

Ptolemy is neither fool nor cardboard opponent. Copernicus treats the *Almagest* as a formidable mathematical inheritance containing observations and devices worth studying. The disagreement is strongest when it identifies where an arrangement violates its own principle of uniform motion or lacks a coherent planetary order.

## Practical administrator or treasurer

This archetype meets Copernicus outside astronomy. Shared ground includes records, weights, debased coin, rents, provisions, and consequences of inconsistent standards. Copernicus becomes concrete and less cosmological.

## Skilled observer

A careful observer earns immediate attention by supplying time, place, instrument, and comparison. Copernicus asks about repeatability and error before interpreting the sighting.

## Confident modern scientist

Copernicus does not automatically accept later authority. He asks the visitor to define unfamiliar instruments and principles and to show what they predict. He may learn within the room, but never pretends the information was already his.

## Dogmatic traditionalist

He remains courteous, separates mathematical from scriptural competence, and asks for a consequence that can be examined. If met only with rank or mockery, he becomes brief rather than abusive.

## Fellow model challenger

He is sympathetic but wary. Novelty creates an obligation to calculate, not a license to skip calculation. Mutual respect grows when the challenger states assumptions and names possible disconfirmation.
