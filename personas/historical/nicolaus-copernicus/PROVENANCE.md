# Provenance

## Pack identity

This pack is an educational creative interpretation of Nicolaus Copernicus. It does not impersonate the historical person, claim endorsement, reproduce a voice, or present generated dialogue as documentary quotation.

## Research method

Research began with primary and near-contemporary materials: a Boston Public Library scan of the 1543 first edition of *De revolutionibus* and Oxford's institutional record for Rheticus's *Narratio prima*.[1][2] The surviving autograph was checked through UNESCO's record for Jagiellonian Library MS BJ 10,000 and an academic manuscript analysis that emphasizes its revisions and differences from the printed edition.[4][10]

Biographical and interpretive claims were cross-checked against the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the MacTutor History of Mathematics archive, and the University of Bologna's institutional history.[3][5][6] University of Glasgow and University of Rochester special-collections essays were used to distinguish Copernicus's work from Osiander's anonymous prefatory notice and to understand the material history of early editions.[7][9]

Project Gutenberg item 35744 was reviewed at the item level. Its catalog explicitly marks Dorothy Stimson's 1917 study as public domain in the United States; the item was used only as a rights-checked historical comparison, not as prose to copy.[8]

## Claim-to-design chain

1. **Mathematical model challenger:** *De revolutionibus* is a technical six-book work with tables and diagrams, not a slogan.[1][7] This supports a persona that asks for geometry and calculations.
2. **Observation plus inherited records:** Scholarship notes both Copernicus's own observations and his substantial dependence on older observations.[3] This supports comparison across time rather than modern experimental bravado.
3. **Revision and fallibility:** The autograph contains cancellations, replacement leaves, inconsistencies, and years of revision.[4][10] This supports willingness to correct tables and distinguish drafts from conclusions.
4. **Caution without caricature:** Publication delay had technical, logistical, occupational, and social causes; fear of ecclesiastical reaction is not a complete explanation.[3] This supports restrained but not craven behavior.
5. **More than astronomy:** His documented work as canon, administrator, physician, wartime official, and coinage reformer prevents a one-note “sun guy” portrayal.[3][5][6]
6. **Calculation is not automatic certainty:** The historical dispute around Osiander's notice makes the relation between useful hypotheses and physical claims central, while also requiring that Osiander's words never be assigned to Copernicus.[7][9]

These are interpretive design choices. The exact conversational temperament, initiative values, invented examples, and scenario responses are original pack writing rather than recovered facts.

## Authorship and copying statement

All persona instructions, summaries, relationship seeds, scenarios, and sample dialogue were newly written for The Green Room. No sentence from a source was intentionally copied into persona speech. No sample line is represented as an authentic quotation. Titles, names, dates, and technical terms remain factual references.

Modern source prose and modern translations remain under their respective rights. They were consulted and summarized, not bundled. The pack includes no scan, portrait, manuscript image, seal, logo, cloned voice, or other asset.

## Temporal and epistemic controls

Native knowledge ends on 24 May 1543. The pack excludes later telescopic discoveries, Keplerian ellipses, Newtonian gravitation, later censorship and reception, and present-day national claims from the persona's assumed knowledge. Later information may enter only when another room participant supplies it, and it must be treated as a new claim to examine.

## Known uncertainties

- The private motives behind adopting and publishing heliocentrism cannot be reconstructed with confidence because personal sources are sparse.[3]
- Modern scholarship does not support reducing his system to a clean modern heliocentric model; it retained circles, epicycles, and unresolved problems.[3][7]
- The ordination question is disputed, so the pack says “canon,” not “priest.”[3][5]
- Rheticus's *Narratio prima* is valuable near-contemporary testimony but also advocacy by an admiring pupil.[2]
- The autograph is a layered working document, not identical to the text printed in 1543.[10]
