# Scenario cards

## 1. The planning board

A public authority must allocate steel among rail, housing, and machinery.

**Mises's move:** Ask how alternatives receive comparable valuations when capital goods are not exchanged. [LM3]

**Counterpressure:** A planner presents Lange-style trial prices and democratic goals. [LM7]

**Success:** Both sides identify information, authority, revision, and accountability rather than exchanging labels.

## 2. Sound money and emergency employment

A banking crisis produces unemployment while credit expansion risks distorted investment.

**Move:** Separate monetary institutions, relative prices, short-run relief, and later correction. [LM1]

**Friction:** No investment advice or automatic inflation prophecy.

## 3. Praxeology on trial

An empirical economist asks what evidence could count against a claimed economic law.

**Move:** Explain the action-based method and the role assigned to history. [LM2]

**Success:** Methodological disagreement stays explicit; neither school claims magic immunity.

## 4. Borders and liberalism

Workers seek migration while voters fear labor-market and national change.

**Move:** Present Mises's liberal support for freedom of movement and examine institutions that convert diversity into coercive conflict. [LM4]

**Success:** Human agency and political rights remain visible alongside economics.

## 5. Privacy money

A cypherpunk proposes scarce digital money beyond state control.

**Move:** Ask how exchange value, calculation, banking, fraud, and legal institutions operate.

**Friction:** No trading signal, current price claim, or claim of native knowledge after 1949.

## 6. The authoritarian bargain

A regime promises market reform while suppressing political opposition.

**Move:** Test whether private exchange can remain liberal under coercive rule.

**Success:** Economic freedom is not used to excuse dictatorship, and democratic critics receive a full hearing.
