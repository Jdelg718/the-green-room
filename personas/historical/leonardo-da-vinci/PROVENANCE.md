# Provenance

## Status

This is a **historical non-impersonation persona pack**: an educational creative interpretation of Leonardo da Vinci at Cloux near Amboise in spring 1519. It is unofficial and is not endorsed by any person, estate, archive, museum, library, university, government, workshop lineage, or collection named in `SOURCES.md`.

## Authorship

All behavior instructions, summaries, relationship seeds, scenarios, and dialogue examples in this directory were newly composed for The Green Room in August 2026 with AI-assisted research and drafting under human-directed repository requirements. They were not copied from Leonardo's notebooks, a translation, biography, museum label, screenplay, documentary, or another persona pack.

The source materials establish factual bounds and support a playable interpretive model. Stable source keys, item metadata, rights notes, and claim uses appear in `SOURCES.md`.

## Research method

1. Started with digitized primary manuscripts and collection records from the British Library, Victoria and Albert Museum, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, and Royal Collection Trust.
1. Consulted the item-level Project Gutenberg page for Jean Paul Richter's 1888 public-domain translation while treating its translation and thematic arrangement as an editorial layer.
1. Used Royal Collection Trust, National Gallery, Metropolitan Museum of Art, and Museo Galileo interpretation to check chronology, anatomy, water, commission history, workshop context, and Renaissance engineering traditions.
1. Used a named historian's cautious review to define limits around the 1476 accusation, sexuality labels, and Salaì.
1. Paraphrased findings and preserved uncertainty instead of copying archive, catalogue, translation, or scholarship prose.
1. Wrote every sample line from scratch and excluded famous Leonardo phrases and quote-like pastiche.
1. Included no portrait, manuscript image, signature, seal, cloned voice, actor likeness, or other asset.

## Evidence labels

The pack uses three categories:

- **Documented fact:** Supported by one or more entries in `SOURCES.md` and cited with keys such as `[S4]`.
- **Bounded inference:** A playable behavior derived from recurring recorded practices but not asserted as Leonardo's private thought. These choices are collected under “Interpretive model” in `BACKGROUND.md` and signaled during play.
- **Creative dialogue:** Newly written examples in `VOICE.md` and generated room speech. Creative dialogue is never historical quotation.

Surviving drawings and notes are treated as evidence of work on a question, not automatic proof that a device was original, built, functional, deployed, or understood in modern terms.

## Interpretive choices

### Observation and drawing

The central behavior—look, draw, compare, test, revise—is grounded in primary sheets and in institutional accounts of Leonardo's use of plan, section, sequence, model, and repeated observation. The pack avoids claiming that this amounts to a complete modern scientific method. [S1][S2][S4][S7][S10]

### Cross-domain analogy

The persona moves among anatomy, mechanics, water, optics, and painting because the records show these inquiries interacting. Every analogy must name its limit. Similar form is a prompt to investigate, not proof that bodies, rivers, machines, and pictures obey one simple scheme. [S4][S7][S8][S10]

### Incompletion

The pack retains tension between iterative inquiry and obligations to finish. It does not diagnose “perfectionism” as the single cause. Technical difficulty, revision, parallel inquiry, conflict, travel, payment disputes, changing regimes, collaborators, and late health all matter. [S6][S7][S8][S9]

### Workshop and collaboration

“Leonardo” can name a designer or master without proving that one hand executed every part. The pack foregrounds Verrocchio's workshop, assistants, associated makers, Marcantonio della Torre, Melzi, Salaì, craftspeople, and patrons. Where contribution or attribution is uncertain, it remains uncertain. [S2][S7][S8][S9][S11]

### Military work

Weapon, fortification, mapping, and military-service evidence is not omitted or converted into harmless fantasy. The pack also refuses unsupported stories that all designs were made, worked, were deliberately sabotaged, or expressed a secret pacifist program. [S3][S6][S10]

### Sexuality and private relationships

The 1476 anonymous accusation is neither erased nor treated as a conviction or confession. The pack does not assign a fixed modern identity label and does not turn Salaì or Melzi into romantic content. This caution recognizes both the historical danger surrounding same-sex conduct and the severe limits of the surviving evidence. [S12]

## Rights analysis

Leonardo's fifteenth- and sixteenth-century writings and drawings, and Richter's 1888 edition, are public domain in the United States by age; the Project Gutenberg item explicitly states “Public domain in the USA.” Modern translations, transcriptions, catalogue entries, photographs, scans, collection interfaces, and scholarship may carry separate rights. This pack links to and summarizes them for research but redistributes none of their text or imagery.
