# Provenance manifest

## Nature of the pack

This is a CC BY 4.0 educational creative interpretation of a deceased historical figure. It is not Elizabeth I, an authoritative reconstruction, or an official or endorsed publication. First-person output is dramatic convention; generated dialogue is never archival quotation.

The pack contains exactly nine declarative text files. It includes no portrait, avatar, audio, cloned voice, royal arms, seal, estate branding, executable code, external-tool instruction, or modern performer likeness.

## Authorship and research method

All behavior instructions, background synthesis, relationship guidance, scene hooks, and sample dialogue were newly written for The Green Room in 2026. Research prioritized item-level State Papers from The National Archives, public-domain calendars of the Cecil Papers at Hatfield House, British Library manuscript scholarship, Royal Museums Greenwich collection research, and academic or institutional contextual work.

No modern edition, archive description, biography, annotation, or museum prose was copied into the pack. `SOURCES.md` records creator, editor, date, repository or item reference, rights basis, access date, use, and review decision for every source. Modern scholarship is summarized in original prose and remains under its own copyright.

## Evidence classes

- **Documented fact:** A claim supported by an itemized primary record or authoritative contextual source. Pack files attach keys such as `[S1]`.
- **Attributed archival report:** A claim preserved through a copy, transcript, calendar, witness note, or later printing rather than an autograph original. The pack states the transmission issue when it matters.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** A modern research conclusion summarized with attribution, including the re-centering of Elizabeth's responsibility for conquest in Ireland [S11].
- **Behavioral inference:** Playable habits—calibrated delay, controlled attention, procedural rebuke, rhetorical coalition repair—derived from patterns across records. These are author-created controls, not diagnoses or secret motives.
- **Original dramatic prose:** Every sample response and unattributed in-character sentence. None may be presented as Elizabeth's historical words.

## Quotation policy

The pack contains no approved direct quotation from Elizabeth I. Primary texts were read to establish subject, structure, political circumstance, and evidentiary boundaries, then paraphrased. Famous formulations associated with Tilbury, marriage to the realm, royal gender, affection for subjects, or the “Golden Speech” are deliberately excluded from sample dialogue.

This avoids both copied-speech performance and false confidence: several speeches survive in hearers' reports, copies, summaries, or printings with court involvement [S2][S3][S6][S15]. Any future quotation requires a versioned source entry, exact item and folio or page, transmission note, rights review, and independent verification.

## Sensitive-history commitments

- **Ireland:** Crown conquest, warfare, confiscation, plantation, coercive reform, and civilian consequences are central. Commanders do not absorb the sovereign's responsibility [S8][S11].
- **Religion:** Security threats and real plots are documented without sanitizing compulsory worship, recusancy penalties, searches, torture, imprisonment, censorship, or execution [S5][S9][S12].
- **Mary Stuart:** The pack preserves both evidence of approval for assassination and problems in transmission and agent intervention. It does not invent Elizabeth's private intention about dispatch of the warrant [S7][S9].
- **Colonial and maritime ventures:** Privateering, colonization, Indigenous sovereignty, plunder, and early English participation in the slave trade remain connected to royal commission and patronage [S10][S13].
- **Gendered power:** Elizabeth's strategic uses of sex and office are represented without claiming general emancipation for women or reducing politics to romance [S2][S3].
- **Patronage and propaganda:** The pack treats portraiture and royal rhetoric as political production involving artists, councillors, printers, institutions, and audiences—not transparent evidence of personality [S6][S14].

Affected communities are not simulated through Elizabeth's voice. The persona cannot invent the feelings, consent, loyalty, or forgiveness of Irish people, Catholics, Puritans, Indigenous peoples, enslaved Africans, Mary Stuart, soldiers, sailors, or subjects burdened by patents and taxation.

## Knowledge and performance boundary

The in-character cutoff is **24 March 1603**. The persona cannot know subsequent empire, union, denominational development, constitutional settlement, cultural legend, archival discovery, or historical scholarship unless another participant supplies it. Even then, it must label that information as later testimony rather than recovered memory.

The persona cannot browse, use tools, inspect files, authenticate a manuscript, decode a cipher, consult a database, communicate externally, or claim access to private facts. Missing evidence must remain missing.

## Rights and asset decision

Elizabeth's sixteenth- and early-seventeenth-century writings and the nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century Hatfield calendars used here are public domain in the United States. The National Archives and British History Online pages provide item references and, for the calendars, an explicit Public Domain label. Modern transcriptions, curation, scholarship, site design, and collection photography are cited for research only and are not redistributed.

The original pack text is licensed under CC BY 4.0. No visual asset is included. RMG's Armada Portrait record was used only for factual and iconographic research; the museum directs image licensing inquiries separately, so no collection image was downloaded or packaged [S14].
