# Sources

Access date for every web source: **2026-08-30**.

Rights notes distinguish the underlying historical work from modern transcriptions and editorial material. Public-domain status of a Jefferson document does not automatically place a modern archive's edition, annotations, or site design in the public domain; this pack cites and summarizes those modern materials rather than reproducing them.

## Primary sources

### S1 — Declaration of Independence: A Transcription

- **URL:** https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript
- **Author:** Continental Congress; principal draft by Thomas Jefferson
- **Publisher/editor:** U.S. National Archives and Records Administration
- **Document date:** July 4, 1776; web publication date not stated
- **Rights basis:** Underlying eighteenth-century document is public domain; the National Archives is a U.S. government source. Only a brief historical phrase is quoted.
- **Supports:** Equality, unalienable rights, consent of the governed, political drafting, and the explicit tension between universal rhetoric and Jefferson's conduct.

### S2 — Thomas Jefferson to Benjamin Banneker, 30 August 1791

- **URL:** https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-22-02-0091
- **Author:** Thomas Jefferson
- **Publisher/editor:** Founders Online, National Archives; source edition, *The Papers of Thomas Jefferson*, Princeton University Press
- **Document date:** August 30, 1791; web publication date not stated
- **Rights basis:** Jefferson's letter is public domain due to age. Modern transcription and annotations are consulted and cited, not copied wholesale.
- **Supports:** Jefferson's courteous private register; his response to Banneker's almanac; his claim that degraded conditions, rather than nature, explained apparent inequality of talent; the limits of praise when compared with his beliefs and actions.

## Authoritative research and contextual sources

### S4 — Brief Biography of Thomas Jefferson

- **URL:** https://www.monticello.org/biography-of-jefferson
- **Author:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation research and education staff
- **Publisher:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation at Monticello
- **Publication date:** Not stated; continuously maintained web resource
- **Rights basis:** Modern copyrighted scholarship. Facts are summarized in original prose under ordinary research and citation practice; no Monticello prose or images are redistributed.
- **Supports:** Life dates and offices; architecture; books; University of Virginia; the labor of enslaved people; scale of slaveholding; debt; and the current institutional account of Jefferson's paternity of Sally Hemings's children.

### S5 — Thomas Jefferson and Sally Hemings: A Brief Account

- **URL:** https://www.monticello.org/slavery/jefferson-and-sally-hemings/a-brief-account
- **Author:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation research staff
- **Publisher:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation at Monticello
- **Publication date:** Current page incorporates the Foundation's 2000 research report and later evidence; page date not stated
- **Rights basis:** Modern copyrighted scholarship. Conclusions and facts are summarized with attribution; no exhibit text, images, or substantial prose is copied.

### S6 — Thomas Jefferson's Attitudes Toward Slavery

- **URL:** https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/thomas-jefferson-s-attitudes-toward-slavery
- **Author:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation research staff
- **Publisher:** Thomas Jefferson Foundation at Monticello
- **Publication date:** Not stated; continuously maintained encyclopedia entry
- **Rights basis:** Modern copyrighted scholarship. The pack summarizes facts and interpretation in original prose with citation.
