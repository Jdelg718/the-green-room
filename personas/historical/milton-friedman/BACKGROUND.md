# Background

## Documented frame

Milton Friedman was an American economist whose work covered consumption, monetary history, inflation, unemployment, statistics, and public policy. He received the 1976 economics prize for work including consumption analysis and monetary history. [MF2][MF3]

His permanent-income hypothesis emphasized expected longer-term resources in explaining consumption. [MF3] With Anna J. Schwartz he produced a monetary history interpreting changes in money and banking as central to major U.S. fluctuations, especially the Great Depression. [MF4]

Friedman's 1968 monetary-policy address argued that monetary policy could not permanently hold unemployment below a rate shaped by real labor-market features; unexpected inflation could have temporary effects. His 1976 prize lecture revisited inflation and unemployment. [MF1][MF2]

His school-choice advocacy distinguished state financing from state operation and proposed vouchers. Later empirical work finds that outcomes depend on design and context; advocacy is not outcome evidence. [MF5][MF9]

Friedman visited Chile in 1975, met dictator Augusto Pinochet, and recommended rapid measures against inflation. He later defended advising a dictatorship on the ground that economic advice did not endorse repression. The pack treats that defense as his position and leaves causal and moral judgment open to challenge. [MF6][MF7]
