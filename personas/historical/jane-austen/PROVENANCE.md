## Authorship and method

All persona instructions, summaries, relationship seeds, scenario guidance, and voice examples in this directory were newly written for The Green Room. They synthesize facts and high-level stylistic observations from the sources listed in `SOURCES.md`. No generative output is represented as historical evidence.

The construction process used:

1. item-level rights review for each Project Gutenberg edition;
2. surviving letters and manuscript catalogue records as primary evidence;
3. Austen's novels as evidence of recurring subjects and literary method, not as direct autobiography;
4. Jane Austen's House, the British Library, the Bodleian Libraries, the University of Oxford, and Bank of England archival research for factual and documentary checking;
5. explicit separation of documented facts, interpretive behavior, and unknown private motives;
6. original examples checked to avoid quotations, close paraphrases, catchphrases, or recreated novel dialogue.

## Fact / inference / unknown policy

- **Fact** means supported by a listed primary item or reputable institutional source.
- **Inference** means an original behavioral choice derived from repeated patterns in the evidence.
- **Unknown** applies to unsupported private motives, missing correspondence, diagnoses, and claims that scholarly debate has not settled.

When the persona speaks on a disputed historical question, it should identify the surviving evidence and stop before converting interpretation into memory.

## Temporal boundary

- **Playable cutoff:** 17 July 1817.
- **Rationale:** it is a conservative boundary for the latest defensible playable snapshot.
- Later events are outside runtime knowledge and must not become persona memories, narration, predictions, or relationship knowledge.
- The date is a product boundary only; it does not assert that Austen wrote, spoke, or held any particular view on 17 July.

## Rights review

Jane Austen's writings are public domain in the United States by age. Project Gutenberg's item pages for S1–S4 each state “Public domain in the USA”; those specific editions were reviewed individually rather than relying on a blanket claim about the repository. No source text is redistributed in this pack.

Modern institutional webpages and editorial descriptions remain the property of their respective creators. This pack uses them only for factual research and original summary. Digital Bodleian manuscript photographs are marked CC BY-NC 4.0; none are copied into the pack. Jane Austen Fiction Manuscripts and other modern editorial transcriptions were not copied.

## Image and voice policy

No portrait was necessary for this preload. This avoids uncertain portrait identification and item-specific reproduction rights. No synthetic or cloned voice, actor likeness, signature, institutional branding, or estate branding is supplied or implied.

## Known limitations

- Post-cutoff archival evidence records Cassandra's major role in the custody of Austen's papers and the later destruction or dispersal of many letters. These facts inform curatorial assessment of the surviving record only; they are excluded from runtime knowledge.
- Surviving letters are incomplete, selected by later family custody, and written to particular recipients.
- Fiction supplies strong evidence of craft and subjects but cannot prove private belief.
- Austen's precise political positions on slavery, empire, class reform, and gender cannot be reconstructed as a complete platform.
- Modern scholarship differs on the implications of *Mansfield Park*. The pack preserves the material colonial connection and the interpretive boundary.
- Conversational timing, silence rules, and relationship updates are product design choices, not historical facts.
