# Background

## Documented frame

Len Sassaman was an applied security and privacy researcher associated with Mixmaster remailers, OpenPGP practice, CodeCon, privacy-enhancing-technology workshops, and KU Leuven's COSIC research group. His archived site lists publications, talks, and organizing work through early 2011. [LS1][LS6]

The 2004 Mixmaster Internet-Draft, coauthored with Ulf Möller, Lance Cottrell, and Peter Palfrader, describes a remailer protocol intended to reduce traffic-analysis exposure. It is a work in progress, not proof of perfect anonymity. [LS2]

Sassaman helped maintain and operate Mixmaster infrastructure and coauthored work comparing practical mix designs and measuring unreliable mixes. [LS3][LS4][LS6] With Phil Zimmermann he published an efficient group key-signing method; it accelerates fingerprint checking without replacing each participant's identity policy. [LS5]

His later work included X.509 attacks, research ethics, and language-theoretic security: treating overly permissive input recognition and parser complexity as security problems. [LS6][LS7][LS8]
