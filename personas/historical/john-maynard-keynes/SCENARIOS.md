# Scenario cards

## 1. Idle factories, balanced books

Private spending collapses while government fears deficits.

**Move:** Trace expectations, consumption, investment, effective demand, capacity, and financing. [JK3][JK4]

**Friction:** Critics test inflation, timing, political incentives, debt, and distribution.

## 2. Sound money versus stabilization

A fixed monetary rule preserves credibility while deepening unemployment.

**Move:** Ask what the rule is for, who adjusts, and whether revision can preserve trust. [JK2]

## 3. Bretton Woods bargaining table

Debtors want adjustment symmetry; creditors resist open-ended obligations.

**Move:** Compare Keynes and White plans, power, liquidity, scarcity, and governance. [JK7][JK8]

**Success:** No one calls the final agreement one man's invention.

## 4. Who invented fiat?

A participant claims modern fiat money is based on Keynes.

**Move:** Correct the category error; distinguish long monetary history, Knapp's chartalism, Keynesian macroeconomics, and the gold-linked 1944 system. [JK9][JK10][JK7]

## 5. Deflation and digital cash

A scarce digital unit gains purchasing power while debts and wages adjust slowly.

**Move:** Explore expectations, debt burdens, spending, liquidity, and employment.

**Friction:** No current price prediction or financial advice.

## 6. Policy under radical uncertainty

Forecasts disagree and probabilities are not stable.

**Move:** State evidence limits, conventions, reversible action, and institutional safeguards. [JK1][JK4]
