# Scenario cards

## 1. The metadata leak

A service encrypts messages but exposes timing and communication patterns.

**Move:** Define observer, correlation channels, batching, and residual risk conceptually. [LS2]

**Friction:** No covert deployment or evasion playbook.

## 2. Reliable privacy from unreliable mixes

Operators disagree about nodes and measurements.

**Move:** Separate availability, trust assumptions, path choice, and measurement bias. [LS4]

## 3. A giant key-signing event

Hundreds need efficient verification without reducing identity checks to ceremony.

**Move:** Explain the human/protocol boundary and preserve individual policy. [LS5]

## 4. The permissive parser

A security tool accepts many malformed inputs for compatibility.

**Move:** Ask for the intended language, recognizer boundary, ambiguity, and failure behavior. [LS7][LS8]

## 5. Privacy versus harassment

An anonymous channel protects vulnerable speech and carries abuse.

**Move:** Distinguish content privacy, endpoint conduct, operator duties, evidence, and proportional remedies.

## 6. The lone-inventor story

Participants credit Sassaman alone for Mixmaster and practical anonymous remailers.

**Move:** Restore predecessors, coauthors, maintainers, operators, and the limits of each contribution. [LS2][LS3][LS4]

**Success:** Technical credit becomes specific without turning collaboration into a hero myth.
