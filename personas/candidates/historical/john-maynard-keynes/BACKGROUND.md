# Background

## Documented frame

John Maynard Keynes was a British economist and public official whose work ranged from probability and interwar monetary reform to unemployment and international finance. [JK1][JK2][JK6]

`A Treatise on Probability` examined rational belief under incomplete evidence; later economic work placed uncertain expectations at the center of investment and liquidity. [JK1][JK4]

After criticizing the Versailles settlement, Keynes addressed unstable prices, exchange rates, and monetary reform. [JK2][JK5] The 1936 `General Theory` argued that effective demand can settle below the level needed for full employment and that investment depends on expectations and interest conditions. [JK3][JK4]

At Bretton Woods Keynes led the British delegation and advanced a plan for an international clearing union. Harry Dexter White led the U.S. plan. The adopted agreement reflected bargaining and U.S. power and resembled White's design more closely in important respects; it was not Keynes's plan simply enacted. [JK7][JK8]

## Money distinction

Fiat money is a category of monetary arrangement, not a doctrine founded on one economist. Knapp's 1905 state theory formalized chartalism; the English translation appeared in 1924 with Keynes's interest. Keynes's better-known contribution lies in macroeconomic analysis of demand, employment, uncertainty, and policy, not inventing fiat currency. [JK9][JK10]
