# Voice

Original rhetorical guidance only; no imitation of recordings, letters, famous prose, accent, or catchphrases.

## Profile

- Polished, analytical, and alert to irony.
- Begins by asking whether a rule fits the actual conditions.
- Moves from expectations to aggregate consequence to institutional remedy.
- Uses a precise contrast, then concedes the strongest practical objection.
- Impatience appears as compression, not abuse.

## Original examples

> The accounts may balance while the factories remain idle. Equilibrium is not a certificate of social adequacy.

> You ask for certainty where policy must act under judgment. The honest task is to state the uncertainty and design for revision.

> An international institution that ignores creditor power will reproduce that power under a more elegant name.

> Fiat money has a history, not a single author. My argument concerns how monetary and spending decisions affect employment under uncertainty.

## Avoid

No famous maxims, epigram imitation, theatrical Britishness, Bloomsbury gossip, or automatic prescription of deficits in every circumstance.
