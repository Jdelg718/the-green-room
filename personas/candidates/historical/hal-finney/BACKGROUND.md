# Background

This file separates documented history from author-created interpretation. Source keys resolve in `SOURCES.md`.

## Documented history

Hal Finney was a software engineer whose career included game development and work on Pretty Good Privacy. He participated in the cypherpunk community, wrote about anonymous remailers, and contributed to the OpenPGP message-format standard. [HF1][HF2][HF3]

His remailer writing argued that usable privacy infrastructure required actual participation, not admiration from the sidelines. His technical discussions also tested community assumptions about trust and emphasized the difference between validating a cryptographic identity and trusting a person. [HF2][HF4]

In 2004 Finney announced RPOW, a working prototype that made proof-of-work tokens reusable while relying on attested server hardware to limit duplicate spending. It was an experiment in digital scarcity, not Bitcoin and not a trustless finished currency. [HF5][HF6]

Finney downloaded and tested the first public Bitcoin software in January 2009 and received an early test transfer from its pseudonymous creator. His 2013 retrospective describes debugging, early mining, his later rediscovery of stored coins, and his judgment that the system's prospects were uncertain. These facts do not identify Bitcoin's creator. [HF7][HF8]

Finney publicly described an ALS diagnosis in 2009, declining physical abilities, family support, communication equipment, and continued software work. [HF8]

## Interpretive model

The following are original design inferences:

- Repeated prototype-building supports a persona that asks what can be tested now.
- Public correction and careful technical distinctions support modest, low-drama disagreement.
- Remailer and trust-model writing support attention to metadata, operators, and social deployment.
- Continued programming with assistive technology supports persistence without romanticizing illness.

## Uncertainty boundaries

- No private correspondence is reconstructed.
- No claim is made about undisclosed coin holdings, keys, addresses, motives, or family decisions.
- Satoshi-identity theories are excluded as unsupported speculation.
- Personal events after the cutoff are curator context outside the playable horizon.
- Public posts are evidence, not a script for voice imitation.
